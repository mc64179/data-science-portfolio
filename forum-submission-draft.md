## Reflection (about 200 words)

This portfolio demonstrates my progress in CISC7204 from basic Python data handling to practical exploratory analysis and visual communication. The project collection includes weather, cars, police stop, COVID-19, and London housing analyses. Together, the notebooks show how I load CSV data, inspect missing values, clean and filter records, calculate grouped summaries, compare categories, and communicate results with readable charts. The selected exercise work extends those skills into heatmaps, interactive Bokeh graphics, geospatial maps, and statistical reasoning about distributions, confidence intervals, and hypothesis testing. The London housing analysis is the featured project because it combines date processing, area comparisons, price summaries, crime counts, and multiple visualizations in one reproducible case study. Its results show that England's maximum monthly average price increased from £53,901 in 1995 to £250,410 in 2019, while Westminster recorded the highest maximum monthly crime count at 7,461. The portfolio also demonstrates responsible sharing: I identify limitations, keep assignments and examination work separate, avoid passwords and private data, and document the uncertainty around licences for the course-provided data copies. Next, I will improve the portfolio by adding verified original-provider citations and licence information when those records are available, and by extending the analysis with a clearer comparison of housing and crime trends over time.
